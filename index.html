About This Map

This map layers Houston’s 1930s federal redlining grades with the freeways built through the city’s Black and Mexican-American neighborhoods, FEMA floodplains, and current Census income and race data, so you can see where historical decisions and today’s conditions line up.

Data sources

  • Redlining: Home Owners’ Loan Corporation map of Houston, dated 1937 in the Big Ten Academic Alliance catalog. Polygons from Mapping Inequality (Robert K. Nelson, LaDale Winling, et al., University of Richmond; CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), via its census-tract crosswalk.
  • Freeways: Simplified route lines, drawn only within the study area; dates and neighborhoods from the Baker Institute, Erik Slotboom’s Houston Freeways, AARoads and TxDOT history. Sources are linked on each freeway.
  • Income, poverty, race & ethnicity: U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey 2018–2022 5-year estimates, by census tract. Suppressed estimates are shown as “No data”.
  • Flood zones: FEMA National Flood Hazard Layer (extract; may be incomplete — check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for any specific address).
  • Capital Flow: Zillow Research Home Value Index (ZHVI) and Observed Rent Index (ZORI) by ZIP, through Aug 2026; Census ACS 2020–24 5-year estimates by ZIP Code Tabulation Area via Census Reporter. Descriptive measures only — no composite risk score.
  • Mortgage lending: Home Mortgage Disclosure Act loan records from the CFPB HMDA Data Browser, by 2020 census tract. Home ownership (in Capital Flow): Harris Central Appraisal District 2026 certified roll. Owner types come from published keyword rules; large operators are matched by holding-company name or the operator's own office address; company-held homes built in the last two years (mostly developer inventory) are left out. Method and a check against the Kinder Institute's 2026 count: scripts/capital/METHOD.md.
  • Political districts: Census TIGERweb, Harris County and City of Houston boundaries; officeholders as of Sept 27, 2026 from official rosters (see data/political/officeholders.json).

The timeline cards and freeway layer change by decade. Census layers show a single recent period and do not change with the timeline. HOLC maps recorded lending judgments that the FHA and private lenders already applied; historians debate how much the maps themselves caused.
